`pyrabind` includes a head-to-head benchmark suite (`benchmarks/`) comparing:

- **Pure C++ `-O3`** (`benchmarks/bench_cpp.cpp`)
- **Python `pyrabind`** (`benchmarks/bench_module.cpp` passing straight through to upstream `nanobind` with `NOMINSIZE` / `-O3`)
- **Raw `nanobind`** (`benchmarks/raw_nb_baseline.cpp` using `NB_MODULE` directly with `NOMINSIZE` / `-O3`, covering all 7 workloads plus `std::vector` copy)
- **Raw Python C-API** (`benchmarks/raw_py_baseline.cpp` using `METH_FASTCALL` + `Py_buffer`)
- **R `pyrabind`** (`benchmarks/bench_module.cpp` compiled against `cpp11` + `.External` + hashed class environments)
- **Raw R `.Call`** (`benchmarks/raw_r_baseline.cpp`)
- **Pure NumPy / pandas & pure base R**

## Test Environment

Measured in full `-O3` Release mode (`-DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release` with `NOMINSIZE` passed to `pyrabind_add_module` and `nanobind_add_module`) on:

- **Hardware / OS**: `Apple M4`, `macOS-27.0.1-arm64-arm-64bit` (`arm64`)
- **Compiler**: `Apple clang version 21.0.0 (clang-2100.4.1.1)` (`-std=c++20`)
- **Python stack**: Python `3.12.8`, `nanobind 3.1.0`, NumPy `2.4.4`, pandas `3.0.2`
- **R stack**: R `4.5.3`, `cpp11 0.5.2`

*(Note: passing `NOMINSIZE` overrides `nanobind`'s default `-Os` size-optimization flag so scalar loops and C++ dispatch compile with `-O3` identically across `pyrabind_bench_cpp`, `bench_module`, `raw_nb_bench`, `raw_py_bench`, and `raw_r_bench`.)*

## 1. Core FFI Dispatch & Class Methods (Median Latency)

| Benchmark | C++ `-O3` | Python `pyrabind` | Raw `nanobind` | `pyrabind` / `nb` | Python raw C-API | R `pyrabind` | R raw `.Call` | `pyrabind` / raw (R) |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `noop()` | 0.5 ns | **14.9 ns** | 14.4 ns | **1.03x** | 10.4 ns | 304.7 ns | 113.6 ns | 2.68x |
| `add_i64(17, 25)` | 0.4 ns | **32.3 ns** | 32.0 ns | **1.01x** | 27.1 ns | 396.7 ns | 146.1 ns | 2.71x |
| `BenchPoint.dot` | 1.2 ns | **36.3 ns** | 36.0 ns | **1.01x** | 27.5 ns | 2.24 us | 186.7 ns | 11.98x |

Caching the S3 `$` method lookup once in R (`pdot <- p1$dot; pdot(p2)`) reduces R `BenchPoint.dot` from **2.24 us** to **476.4 ns** (**2.55x** vs raw `.Call`).

**Takeaway on Python**: Because `pyrabind` re-exports `::nanobind` directly without wrapper objects or extra indirection, dispatch latency sits at **0.98x-1.03x** of raw `nanobind` (**14.9 ns** `noop`, **32.3 ns** `add_i64`, **36.3 ns** `point_dot`), within **5-10 ns** of raw hand-written `METH_FASTCALL` CPython C-API.

## 2. NumPy, `ndarray`, Matrices, Arrow & DataFrames (Median Latency)

| Workload | C++ `-O3` | Python `pyrabind` (zero-copy) | Raw `nanobind` (zero-copy) | `pyrabind` / `nb` | `std::vector` copy (`pb` / `nb`) | Python raw C-API | Pure NumPy / pandas / pyarrow | R `pyrabind` (zero-copy) | R `std::vector` copy | R raw `.Call` | Pure base R |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `dot_100k` | 51.13 us | **51.44 us** | 51.32 us | **1.00x** | 468.78 us / 469.48 us | 51.35 us | 3.25 us | **51.46 us** | 76.60 us | 51.41 us | 143.43 us |
| `scale_100k` | 15.82 us | **13.01 us** | 13.14 us | **0.99x** | 1.000 ms / 993.53 us | 12.84 us | 24.79 us | **28.16 us** | 50.14 us | 15.62 us | 51.86 us |
| `mat_sum_256` | 32.25 us | **32.46 us** | 32.49 us | **1.00x** | -- | 32.67 us | 8.09 us | **32.90 us** | -- | 32.47 us | 75.41 us |
| `df_transform_100k` | 127.09 us | 4.627 ms | 346.62 us | 13.35x | -- | 309.94 us | 430.38 us | 1.973 ms | -- | 19.33 us | 176.61 us |
| `arrow_transform_100k` | 24.14 us | **26.33 us** | **26.79 us** | **0.98x** | -- | -- | 65.53 us (`pyarrow`) | **98.90 us** | -- | 70.47 us | -- |

<Aside type="note" title="Zero-Copy Arrow vs. Eager data_frame Materialization">
1. **`arrow_transform_100k` achieves full raw `nanobind` parity (`26.33 us` vs `26.79 us`, `0.98x` — 2.49x faster than pure `pyarrow` and 175x faster than eager `df_transform_100k`)**: Using `nb::arrow_batch_view` (`column_span<T>` + `insert_column`) inspects `id` and `x` directly from borrowed Arrow buffers and passes untouched columns (`id`, `x`, and all 100k `tag` strings) through with zero bytes copied. In R, `arrow_transform_100k` runs in **98.90 us** (compared to **1.973 ms** for eager `data_frame`, **20x faster**).
2. **Why `df_transform_100k` (`4.627 ms` / `1.973 ms`) differs from `raw_nb_baseline` (`346.62 us`) and `raw_r_baseline` (`19.33 us`)**: The raw baselines pass `id`, `x`, and the 100k-element `tag` string column through untouched by host handle without inspecting or copying strings. `pyrabind::data_frame` eagerly converts all 4 columns (2x100k `std::string`/`PyUnicode`/`CHARSXP` allocations) so user C++ code works solely with `std::vector` and `validity` masks. Choose `nb::arrow_batch_view` or `nb::ndarray` whenever tabular transformations touch large string columns.
</Aside>

### Key Findings

1. **Identical Python performance to upstream `nanobind` across every zero-copy workload**:
   - With `-O3` (`NOMINSIZE`), `dot_100k` reaches **51.44 us** in `pyrabind` vs **51.32 us** in raw `nanobind` (`1.00x`).
   - `scale_100k` runs in **13.01 us** (`pyrabind`) / **13.14 us** (raw `nanobind`) via zero-copy `nb::ndarray` + `nb::capsule` ownership transfer — faster than pure vectorized NumPy (**24.79 us**) and **~76x faster** than returning `std::vector<double>` (**1.000 ms**).
   - `mat_sum_256` matches raw `nanobind` and raw CPython C-API (**32.46 us** vs **32.49 us** / **32.67 us**, `1.00x`).
   - `arrow_transform_100k` reaches **26.33 us** in `pyrabind` vs **26.79 us** in raw `nanobind` (`0.98x`) and **24.14 us** in pure C++ `-O3`.
2. **Zero-copy R matrices, vectors & Arrow batches match native C**:
   - `dot_100k` (**51.46 us** vs **51.41 us** raw `.Call`, **2.79x faster** than base R `143.43 us`) and column-major `mat_sum_256` (**32.90 us** vs **32.47 us** raw `.Call`, **2.29x faster** than base R `75.41 us`) achieve native C speed with zero transposition.
   - `arrow_transform_100k` runs in **98.90 us** in R vs **1.973 ms** for eager `data_frame` copies (**20x speedup**).

## Reproducing Locally

```bash
cmake --preset release
cmake --build build-release -j
python3 benchmarks/run_all.py --build-dir build-release
```

Optionally pass `--python /path/to/python3` and `--json-out results.json`.
